**Patient Assessment – Medical**

| Scene Size-Up |  |
| ----- | :---- |
| PPE \- Personal Protective Equipment  | Gloves, mask etc. |
| Scene Safety  | Survey the scene for any dangers to you or to  the patient. |
| NOI – Nature of Illness / Impression of  Surroundings | NOI – Determine why EMS was activated. General impression of the surroundings – What sort of setting am I in? What is the  general state of the house that I’m in? Are   there others present as well? |
| Number of Patients  | Is this my only patient? |
| Additional Help Needed  | Do I need to call for ALS? (It may not be  evident at this point)  Do I need additional BLS backup?  Do I need fire department? Police? Army? |

| Primary Assessment |  |
| ----- | :---- |
| General Impression  | General impression of the patient’s   appearance and position. |
| LOC – Level of Consciousness  | AVPU:  **A**lert – responsive  **V**erbal – responds to verbal stimuli  **P**ain – responds to painful stimuli   **U**nresponsive – to all stimulation – no gag or  cough reflex |
| Orientation of Patient  | If patient is alert, assess orientation to: Person – what is your name?  Place – where are you right now?  Time – what is the date and time of day? Event – what happened? Why did you call? |

| Chief Complaint / Life Threats  | Are there any apparent Immediate life  threats? What is the patient’s chief   complaint? |
| :---- | ----- |
| **A**irway  | If patient is unconscious, check the mouth for  blockages or secretions  • If you **see** an object remove with your  finger by performing a finger sweep.  **NEVER** preform blind finger sweeps  • If there are secretions, suction the  airway.  • If the patient is vomiting large  amounts, turn their head to the side  to clear the airway.  If the patient is able to talk or cry, they have a  patent airway. |
| **B**reathing  | Get general impression of respiratory rate,  and any signs of difficulty breathing, as well  as paying attention to any audible abnormal  breath sounds.  Normal Respirations – 12-20 breaths NRM:  • If the patient has any trouble   breathing, administer oxygen with a   non-rebreather mask.  BVM:  • If there are less than 8 or more than  30 breaths per minute / breathing is  very shallow, then ventilate with a   BVM, giving one breath every six  seconds.   SpO2- above 94% |

| Circulation  | Pulse:  Get a general impression of the radial pulse,  feeling for rate, rhythm, and strength. Is the pulse fast, slow, or normal?  Is the rhythm regular or not?  Is the pulse strong or weak?  Normal pulse – 60-100 bpm for an adult  Skin:  Color – pale / flushed / cyanotic  Moisture – dry / sweaty  Temperature – cool / warm |
| :---- | :---- |
| Neurological **D**eficiency Assessment – Altered  Mental Status | Check glucose:  Normal – 80-120   Hyperglycemic Emergency – \<400 (may vary  from patient to patient)  Hypoglycemic Emergency – \>60 (may vary  from patient to patient) – Administer oral  glucose.  BE FAST – Stroke Assessment:  Check for:  **B**alance – loss of balance / coordination  **E**yes – pupils unequal or unresponsive to light / abnormal movement / blurred vision **F**ace – facial droop / weakness or numbness  **A**rm drift – unilateral weakness or paralysis  **S**peech – dysarthria / aphasia |
| Patient priority  | Is there a threat to the patient’s life? Does  the patient need immediate transport? **REMEMBER** \- any compromise or threat to  the patient’s ABCs requires calling for ALS |

| History Taking |  |
| ----- | :---- |
| OPQRST – investigating chief complaint  | **O**nset – what were you doing when it started? Did it start suddenly or gradually?  **P**rovocation – does anything make it better or  worse?  **Q**uality – describe the pain – Pressure?  Burning? Sharp?   **R**egion / **R**adiation – where does it hurt?  Does the pain radiate in any direction? **S**everity – rate the pain on a scale of 1-10, 10  being the worst pain you can imagine **T**ime – how long ago did it start and since it  started, has it gotten better or worse? |
| SAMPLE History  | **S**igns & **S**ymptoms – any additional   complaints?  **A**llergies – any known allergies?  **M**edication – Do you take any medications?  (SHOP – street, herbal, OTC, prescriptions) If  you have prescribed medications, have you been taking them?  **P**ast medical history – has this happened  before? Do you have any preexisting  conditions?   **L**ast oral intake – when and what did you  lasty eat or drink?  (If relevant – **L**ast menstrual period)  **E**vents leading up – how were you feeling  prior to the incident?  |

| Secondary Assessment |  |
| ----- | :---- |
| Vital Signs  | Take baseline vitals \-  Check respiratory rate   Check SpO2  Check pulse  Check blood pressure  Normal adult vital signs:  Respirations – 12-20 breaths per minute SpO2 – 94% or more  Pulse – 60-100 bpm  Blood pressure – systolic: 90-120(140) mm Hg  diastolic: 60-90 mm Hg |

| Reassessment |  |
| ----- | :---- |
| How Often?  | Critical patient – every 5 minutes  Stable patient – every 15 minutes |
| Mental Status  | Assess for changes in mental status |
| ABCs  | Reassess ABCs |
| Vital signs  | Reassess vital signs |

| Provide accurate verbal report to arriving EMS unit |
